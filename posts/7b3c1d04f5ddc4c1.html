GE Vernova Delivers First UK-Built Transformer for Eastern Green Link 1

Wed, 21 Oct 2026 06:44:34 GMT

GE Vernova, a global energy technology company, has completed the first in a series of 14 high-voltage transformers for the Eastern Green Link 1 (EGL1) subsea transmission project in the United Kingdom. This significant engineering milestone marks the start of equipment deliveries that will enable one of the country’s largest electrical interconnections to move closer to operation.

The transformer, built in the United Kingdom, is now ready for transport. According to the project timeline, installation at EGL1’s converter stations will begin in July 2027. This delivery is especially notable because it is the first transformer produced domestically for this particular project.

Eastern Green Link 1 is a large-scale subsea transmission initiative designed to carry up to 2 gigawatts (GW) of electricity between converter stations. Subsea transmission systems like EGL1 are crucial for transferring large amounts of electrical power over long distances, particularly in regions where renewable energy is abundant and must be moved to centers of demand. In this context, a converter station is an installation that converts electricity between alternating current (AC) and direct current (DC), enabling efficient long-distance transport.

Transformers are a fundamental component of such infrastructure. They are used to step up (increase) or step down (decrease) voltage levels so that electricity can be transmitted efficiently and safely. For an interconnector like EGL1, large-scale transformers ensure the reliable and effective integration of high-capacity transmission links into the existing grid.

The completion of the first transformer is just the beginning for EGL1. In total, 14 UK-built transformers are planned for deployment across the converter stations of this project. On-site installation is scheduled to commence in mid-2027, reinforcing the timeline for this major infrastructure development. This aligns with wider efforts in the UK to support grid upgrades, the shifting energy landscape, and the continuing integration of renewable power sources.

For engineers and professionals in the power sector, this development highlights several key points:

• The importance of domestic manufacturing capacity for critical grid components. • The scale and complexity involved in coordinating multi-year subsea transmission projects. • The essential role of transformers in ensuring the security and efficiency of high-voltage, long-distance power transmission.

This stage also provides a reference for project tracking and future tendering activities associated with UK grid enhancements. As technologies evolve and demand patterns change, large projects like EGL1 provide crucial lessons for equipment specification, logistics coordination, and regulatory compliance.

Professionals involved with major transmission links, grid integration, or renewable energy delivery should monitor developments at EGL1 and similar interconnector initiatives. These projects represent both engineering challenges and opportunities to support the sustainable modernization of national power infrastructure.
