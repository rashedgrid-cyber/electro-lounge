Illinois Approves ComEd Project for Four Underground 138 kV Transmission Lines and New Substation Near O’Hare

Tue, 27 Oct 2026 08:36:37 GMT

Illinois regulators have approved a new transmission project proposed by ComEd. The plans include constructing four underground transmission lines, each rated at 138 kilovolts (kV), as well as a new substation to be built near O’Hare International Airport. The project is expected to enter service in 2029.

For electrical professionals, understanding the process behind such infrastructure projects is vital. Projects like these are key examples of how utilities plan and implement upgrades to serve urban centers and critical infrastructure hubs. This project specifically demonstrates the use of underground high-voltage lines, which are often selected in dense metropolitan areas where space is limited or where minimizing above-ground disruption is a priority.

General engineering background: Underground transmission lines at 138 kV are used to transmit electricity over relatively long distances within cities or between substations. Specialized insulation and thermal management systems are required to ensure reliability and safe operation below ground. Building a new substation alongside these lines involves precise design to manage incoming high-voltage circuits and distribute power to local distribution networks efficiently.

This development provides a practical case for engineers to examine design, permitting, and scheduling considerations for major urban electrical infrastructure projects.
