Transformer Seismic Design: What Should Substation Engineers Consider?

Thu, 01 Oct 2026 12:00:00 GMT

⚡ General engineering principles — applicable internationally.

When we talk about earthquake-resistant transformers, it is easy to focus on the tank. But the whole installation deserves attention.

A transformer depends on its foundations, anchoring, bushings, supporting structures and connections. Bushings are the insulated terminals that let conductors pass through the equipment enclosure. Flexible connections and cable arrangements also matter because different parts of the installation can move during a seismic event.

For a design review, look at the transformer and its surroundings together: the conservator supports, auxiliary equipment and control cabinets, as well as the main unit. Seismic qualification is about checking whether equipment can withstand the specified earthquake conditions; it is a useful topic to discuss early in the project.

🔎 assess the complete load path—the route through which earthquake forces travel from the equipment into the foundations. Anchors restrain the tank, while bushings and supports must tolerate the specified movement. Connections need an arrangement that accommodates movement without transferring excessive force to terminals. Seismic qualification provides evidence against defined seismic conditions; the required conditions depend on the site and applicable project criteria, rather than assuming that a low-risk region needs no assessment.
