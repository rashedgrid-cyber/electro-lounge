New 250 MW Battery Storage Project Advances Uzbekistan’s Power Infrastructure

Tue, 20 Oct 2026 15:17:18 GMT

In a significant move for Uzbekistan’s electrical grid, UAE-based AMEA Power has finalized the Battery Storage Services Agreement and Investment Agreement for a large-scale Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) in the country’s capital, Tashkent. The project, with a rated capacity of 250 megawatts (MW) and a total storage capability of 1,000 megawatt-hours (MWh), marks one of the latest developments in grid-scale energy storage in Central Asia.

Project Overview and Location The agreement confirms the establishment of a standalone BESS facility in Tashkent, Uzbekistan. This initiative is led by AMEA Power, a developer of renewable energy and energy storage projects headquartered in the United Arab Emirates. The facility’s specifications – 250 MW of instantaneous power and a duration of 1,000 MWh – position it as a major resource for improving grid flexibility and supporting Uzbekistan’s transition towards greater energy reliability.

Understanding Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) are engineered to store excess electricity and release it when demand rises or renewable production falls. The MW (megawatt) rating indicates how much power the system can discharge at any moment, while the MWh (megawatt-hour) value reflects how long it can sustain that output. For instance, in this project, the batteries can deliver 250 MW of electricity over four hours, totaling 1,000 MWh. Such systems are crucial for integrating renewable generation and maintaining stable, reliable power supplies, especially during periods of peak usage or intermittent energy production.

Why This Project Matters for Uzbekistan Uzbekistan’s grid has been undergoing modernization efforts to address both growing demand and increased renewable energy integration. The addition of a large-scale storage asset in Tashkent is expected to enhance load balancing, frequency regulation, and grid resilience. These features are especially important as more variable sources, like wind and solar, contribute to the country’s energy supply. A storage system of this scale provides operators with greater control over energy dispatch and helps to reduce reliance on fossil fuel-based peaking plants.

Strategic Importance for Power Professionals For electrical engineers and power system planners, this agreement signals ongoing regional investment in advanced grid technologies. The deployment of utility-grade BESS infrastructure enables more effective utilization of generated electricity, reduces curtailment of renewables, and supports national energy security goals. Additionally, such projects often serve as blueprints for similar initiatives across neighboring regions, offering important technical lessons for project design, system integration, and operational management.

Project Stage and Industry Context As of the reported date, the agreements have been signed, confirming the next phase of project development and investment. While construction and commissioning timelines were not specified, the signing represents a key project milestone and signals a commitment from both the investor and Uzbekistan’s authorities to move forward with grid-enhancing energy storage installations.

Conclusion The introduction of a 250 MW / 1,000 MWh battery storage facility in Tashkent marks a pivotal development for Uzbekistan’s electrical power sector. By increasing the grid's ability to store and dispatch electricity as needed, power professionals and system operators will be able to manage supply and demand more effectively, accommodate greater renewable integration, and strengthen grid resilience for the future.
