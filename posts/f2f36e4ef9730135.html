Uungula Wind Substation Connects 414 MW Project to the New South Wales Grid

Fri, 23 Oct 2026 08:25:00 GMT

In New South Wales, Australia, a significant milestone has been achieved in renewable energy infrastructure with the energisation of a new wind power substation as part of the Uungula project. The facility, described as a 330 / 33 kilovolt (kV) substation, now connects a major 414 megawatt (MW) wind project to the local grid through nearly 12 kilometers of dedicated transmission line.

Key Players and Location The Uungula substation forms a crucial link in the energy landscape of New South Wales. The project involves organizations such as Squadron Energy and Lumea, both referenced in the context of its development and operation. This grid connection is specifically tailored to enable large-scale renewable energy integration within the region.

Technical Overview The substation is equipped with three power transformers. Transformers play a vital role in adjusting voltage levels for efficient transmission and distribution of electricity. In this case, electricity generated at 33 kV by the wind farm is stepped up to 330 kV for transmission. Higher voltage transmission is beneficial because it reduces energy losses over long distances, allowing more power to be delivered to homes and businesses further away.

The associated nearly 12 km transmission line ensures that the wind-generated power travels securely from the remote generation site to where it's required in the grid. The ability to convert power between voltage levels is foundational to modern electrical networks, supporting both reliability and flexibility.

Project Scale and Purpose With a capacity of 414 MW, the Uungula wind project is an example of large-scale renewable integration. Renewable projects of this capacity help diversify energy sources, support local sustainability goals, and contribute to grid stability.

By providing an efficient way to transfer renewable electricity, the new substation helps address the technical challenges of integrating variable energy sources, such as wind, into the existing power system. Specialized transformer substations like this one can support grid operators by delivering consistent voltage and reliable transmission, even as wind generation fluctuates.

Broader Impact for Power Professionals This development illustrates ongoing investment in modern transmission and substation infrastructure—key interests for electrical engineers, grid operators, and planners. As renewable energy projects scale up, engineering solutions for seamless grid integration become increasingly important.

Power professionals monitoring the deployment of large power transformers, new substation technology, and regional grid upgrades will find the Uungula project relevant as a current, real-world case. Observing such projects provides useful, up-to-date references for transformer specification, grid connection approaches, and overall system planning in the context of renewable energy growth.

The Uungula wind substation stands as a current example of how targeted infrastructure can bridge new generation assets and established grids in the quest for a more sustainable, reliable power system in Australia.
