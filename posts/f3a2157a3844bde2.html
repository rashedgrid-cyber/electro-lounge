National Grid Invests $6.5 Million in Live-Line Maintenance Innovation

Wed, 14 Oct 2026 11:36:53 GMT

⚡ United Kingdom.

National Grid Electricity Transmission in the United Kingdom has secured approximately $6.5 million (£4.95 million) for DELLTA, a three-year project developing methods, equipment, procedures and training for live-line transmission maintenance.

The main focus is replacing overhead-line insulators while the circuit remains energized. An insulator supports a conductor and separates it electrically from the grounded tower. Conventional transmission maintenance is typically carried out during planned outages, with the circuit taken out of service. Reducing those outages could help retain network capacity during maintenance.

Behind the scenes, the earlier Alpha phase demonstrated a proposed insulator-replacement method on a de-energized network. The Beta phase includes specialist training and is expected to conclude with a demonstration on National Grid's transmission network. That distinction matters: an earlier trial is not evidence that live replacement is already routine.

The project team includes Frazer-Nash Consultancy, the University of Manchester, Allied Insulators, the University of Liverpool, SSEN Transmission and Budapest University of Technology and Economics.

🔎 The engineering answer: planning, tools and operating coordination form one safety system. Insulators support conductors while separating them electrically from grounded structures. Replacing or servicing them changes a critical part of that system. Live-line work therefore requires a validated method, specially trained personnel and suitable equipment, with operations teams managing the network conditions. Keeping a circuit energized can retain its transfer capacity during maintenance, but suitability must be assessed for the particular task; it is not an instruction to work on live equipment.
