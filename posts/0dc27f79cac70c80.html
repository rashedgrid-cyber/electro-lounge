Offshore Substations Set Sail for Major Baltic Sea Wind Project

Wed, 14 Oct 2026 10:17:32 GMT

🌊 German sector of the Baltic Sea, Germany.

Two offshore substations are on their way to the German sector of the Baltic Sea for the 927 MW Gennaker wind farm. Each has a capacity of approximately 450 MW.

Think of an offshore substation as a collection point between the wind turbines and the route back to shore. It brings the electrical output together and helps prepare it for transmission to the mainland network. Without that connection infrastructure, generating power offshore is only part of the job.

For electrical engineers, the numbers give a sense of the scale involved. They also lead to practical questions about how equipment, protection, cables and maintenance access fit into an offshore installation. These are discussion points, rather than details confirmed for this particular project.

🔎 The power route is turbines → offshore collection network → offshore substation → export connection → mainland grid. Transformers change the voltage to suit transmission, and protection isolates faults. For the same transmitted power, a higher voltage generally means lower current, which reduces resistive cable losses. The substation and export system must still be designed as one system: headline MW ratings alone do not establish how much power can be transferred under every operating condition. This explains the engineering role without assuming unreported project specifications.
