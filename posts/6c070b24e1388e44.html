Vattenfall to Market 200 MW of Balancing Capacity from Veja Mate Offshore Wind Farm in Germany

Tue, 20 Oct 2026 07:26:48 GMT

Germany’s offshore wind sector marks a significant development with Vattenfall set to market 200 megawatts (MW) of balancing capacity from the 416 MW Veja Mate offshore wind farm. This advancement connects two major players—Vattenfall (a key European energy company) and TenneT (a major transmission system operator)—as part of Germany’s increasing reliance on renewable energy sources.

Project Overview The Veja Mate offshore wind farm, located in the German North Sea, has a total installed capacity of 416 MW. Vattenfall will offer 200 MW of this capacity as balancing power, an important service for electrical grids that helps maintain the real-time balance between supply and demand.

Balancing power, also known as reserve or ancillary services capacity, allows operators to adjust generation or load on short notice. This feature is becoming increasingly vital as the share of intermittent renewable energy—such as wind and solar—grows within national electricity networks.

Why This Matters: The Role of Balancing Power In electrical power systems, maintaining system stability and frequency within defined limits is critical. As more wind generation comes online, grid operators face the challenge of integrating variable outputs into the system. Balancing power acts as a flexible buffer, providing additional generation or reducing output instantly to counteract sudden changes in demand or supply.

By making 200 MW of offshore wind-generated balancing capacity available, Vattenfall supports grid reliability while maximizing the utilization of renewable sources. For engineers, this also highlights the practical steps taken by market operators to enable higher renewable penetration while upholding power quality and security.

Further Technical Context Offshore wind farms like Veja Mate are sited at sea to take advantage of higher and more consistent wind speeds. Integrating these large-scale assets with grid-balancing markets requires coordinated efforts between wind farm operators, transmission system operators like TenneT, and market participants.

Enabling offshore wind to provide ancillary services was once considered technically challenging, but recent advancements in control systems and market frameworks now make it increasingly routine in advanced power systems. This also demonstrates the importance of digitalization, real-time monitoring, and sophisticated forecasting in today’s grid operations.

Country Focus: Germany’s Grid Developments Germany is a leading market in Europe for integrating renewables at scale. The partnership between Vattenfall, TenneT, and Veja Mate shows the country's commitment to reliable, clean energy. Projects like this reinforce how the German power sector is investing in technology and market mechanisms that promote grid stability while scaling up renewables.

For electrical professionals, the news is relevant as it demonstrates how the operational role of offshore wind is evolving. The offering of 200 MW balancing capacity from Veja Mate is a clear example of renewable energy assets stepping into critical system-support functions once dominated by conventional generators.

Key Takeaways for Engineers - Vattenfall will market 200 MW of balancing reserve from Germany’s Veja Mate wind farm - This supports grid stability as more renewable energy is integrated - Collaboration between wind farm operators and transmission system operators like TenneT is essential - Offshore wind’s role is expanding beyond energy supply to grid-balancing

This milestone reflects the ongoing technical and market progress in power systems, making it a noteworthy update for anyone involved in grid operations, system planning, or renewable integration.
