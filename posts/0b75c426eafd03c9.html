Major Federal Backing Advances Alaska Transmission Expansion

Wed, 14 Oct 2026 11:36:53 GMT

🔌 Southcentral and Interior Alaska, United States.

Alaska is planning another high-voltage connection between Southcentral and Interior Alaska. The U.S. Department of Energy has committed support for the $418 million Beluga–Healy transmission project.

The planned line is 359 kilometers, or 223 miles, long and would provide a second high-voltage link between the two regions. It is intended to strengthen reliability and resilience and support substations managed by the Alaska Energy Authority.

🔎 A second transmission route can provide another path for power when one route is unavailable. Transfer capability means how much power the network can carry while remaining within its operating limits. Reliability concerns continuity of supply; resilience concerns withstanding and recovering from disturbances. Another line can help, but the amount of benefit depends on circuit ratings, substations, protection, network conditions and shared failure risks. Two routes affected by the same event are not equivalent to fully independent supplies.
