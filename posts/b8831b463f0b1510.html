Bushings: The Small Component Creating Big Delays in Transformer Delivery

Thu, 22 Oct 2026 11:39:43 GMT

When discussing challenges in the transformer supply chain, much of the focus tends to fall on major materials and components such as turbines, copper, and steel. However, an often-overlooked yet essential component is the transformer bushing. As highlighted by recent updates from GE Vernova, this seemingly minor part has become a critical factor in transformer manufacturing and delivery schedules.

Transformers play a foundational role in electrical power systems, enabling the safe and efficient transfer of electrical energy between circuits. Each transformer typically includes five to seven bushings, which serve as insulation devices for conductors passing into and out of grounded enclosures. Their main function is to isolate high voltage terminals from the grounded tank, ensuring both safety and reliability.

What sets bushings apart from other transformer components is their complete lack of substitutes. There is currently no alternative part or workaround; every power transformer must have its full set of bushings installed before it leaves the factory. If a single bushing is missing, the transformer simply cannot be shipped.

This dependency has significant consequences for project planning and execution. Electric utilities, energy developers, and grid operators may carefully manage supplies of core materials like copper windings and lamination steel, but a shortage of bushings can halt production altogether. Current industry reports indicate that transformer delivery lead times have now stretched to as long as four years. While pricing trends are not publicly detailed in the available information, extended lead times can often mean project delays, increased costs, and logistical challenges for both manufacturers and end-users.

For electrical professionals, this development serves as a crucial reminder. Comprehensive supply chain risk assessments must account for every single critical component—no matter how small or routine it may seem. When even a single part like a bushing becomes a bottleneck, the entire project timeline is impacted. This can affect new grid buildouts, substations, and critical infrastructure upgrades.

It’s also a call to action for professionals involved in procurement, planning, and project management. Ensuring robust supplier relationships, advance ordering, and diversified sourcing of essential components—including bushings—may help to minimize the risk of unforeseen delays. Additionally, recognizing such dependencies early in the planning phase allows stakeholders to better communicate realistic timelines and manage expectations with partners and end users.

In a rapidly evolving power sector with growing demand for modernized grid assets, paying close attention to the supply chain details of vital equipment like transformers is essential. While infrastructure projects tend to attract headlines for their scale, it is the reliability of each part—from the largest steel core to the smallest bushing—that determines completion and operational success.

GE Vernova’s observations highlight a sector-wide challenge with global implications. Every professional working in the power systems domain stands to benefit from this practical insight about transformer bushings—and the crucial role each component plays in maintaining schedule certainty for today’s ambitious grid projects. 🏗️🔌
