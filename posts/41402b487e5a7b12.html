National Grid Initiative Aims to Accelerate Industrial Grid Connections in New South Wales

Sun, 11 Oct 2026 09:32:24 GMT

🔋 New South Wales, Australia.

Getting an industrial site connected to the grid can be a major part of an electrification project. A programme in New South Wales is exploring whether battery storage and flexible connection approaches can help industrial customers connect sooner.

The idea is to make better use of the capacity already available. Batteries can store energy and release it at another time, while a flexible connection can allow the way a site uses the network to adapt to agreed conditions. The programme is testing these approaches; it is not a promise that every site can avoid a network upgrade.

For engineers, the interesting question is how the site's demand, its operating needs and the grid's limits fit together. Faster access is useful only when the connection arrangement also suits the business.

🔎 A simple example: an industrial site's maximum demand may occur for only a few hours. A battery can charge when spare capacity is available and discharge during the peak, reducing the site's demand on the connection. Power in MW describes the rate of delivery; energy in MWh describes how much can be delivered over time. A flexible connection may also require demand to be reduced under agreed conditions. Whether either approach works depends on the process, the duration of peaks and the network agreement.
